Workspace windows can be pulled out of the Mind window into desktop windows of their own.

Drag a window's title bar past the edge of the Mind window and it tears out into a *popout*: a frameless desktop window framed by the workspace's accent, holding that one window edge to edge, that follows the cursor until you let go. "Open in its own window" in the window's menu does the same without a drag. The desktop keeps a ghost of the window where it was.

A popout comes back three ways: drag it over a Mind window showing the same workspace (the surface offers the drop) and release, press "Return to desktop" in its bar, or close it (`Cmd+W` / `Ctrl+W`, the close control, `File > Close Window`), which returns the window to the desktop first. Every return runs through the Mind window showing the workspace (the one dropped onto, else the most recent), whose workspace shell is the desktop's one writer during a drag and a return, so a quick drop back cannot race a save of the popout's own and leave a ghost behind, and a window dragged in and out keeps its place on top of the stack; the popout closes once its own shell sees the window back. Only with no Mind window showing the workspace does the popout's own page take the window back before it goes. Popouts are saved with the other windows and restored at their bounds on relaunch (a popout of a workspace that no longer exists is dropped, like any window of it); a restored popout whose window is gone from the workspace keeps its OS window and shows the ordinary empty state.

Under the hood: the SPA gains the `/popout/<workspace-id>/<window-id>` route and a popout bar in place of the titlebar; the embed contract moves to version 6 with `pop-out-window`, `window-drag-started`, `window-drag-ended`, `detached-windows`, `embedder-capabilities`, `reattach-window`, and `tear-out`; the Electron main process gains a popout window kind (never a navigation target), a watch on every title-bar drag the shell announces (main samples the cursor and opens the popout once it leaves the window by the tear-out distance, since the shell's own pointer events stop at the window's edge on some platforms, such as X11 with an absolute-axis virtual mouse), a cursor-follow loop for the tear-out and the drag back, and a close handshake that returns the window through a main window's shell (`reattach-popout-window`) before the popout goes.
