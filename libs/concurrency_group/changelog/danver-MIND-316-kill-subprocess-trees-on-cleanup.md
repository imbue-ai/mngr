Stopping a process now stops everything that process spawned, instead of only the process itself. `is_own_process_group`, which spawns a child as the leader of a session and process group of its own and signals that group on shutdown, is now on by default for `run_process_in_background`, `run_process_to_completion`, `run_background` and `run_local_command_modern_version`. The group is also SIGKILLed once the child has exited, whether on SIGTERM or on its own, so a wrapper that started work in the background and left does not leave that work running. Previously only the direct child was signalled by default: its descendants were reparented onto init and survived indefinitely, which is the shape behind a family of "a test starts a process that outlives it" CI failures (MIND-248, MIND-313, MIND-314).

The escalation from SIGTERM to SIGKILL now actually happens. `ConcurrencyGroup` allowed all of its cleanup 10 seconds, while the wait before escalating to SIGKILL was 30 -- so a process that did not stop on SIGTERM was abandoned at the 10 second mark, before the kill meant to guarantee its death was ever sent. The default SIGTERM grace (`shutdown_timeout_sec`) is now 3 seconds, and a group waits for the processes it had to terminate to actually die before returning from its context manager, on a budget sized to cover the whole escalation. Exiting a group whose processes overran can therefore take a few seconds longer than it used to, and in exchange those processes are gone when it returns.

A child in a session of its own has no controlling terminal, so a command that must prompt the user there (`sudo` asking for a password) passes `is_own_process_group=False` to stay in the caller's process group. Stopping reaches everything still in the child's group; a daemon that starts a session of its own, such as a tmux server, is left alone as before.
